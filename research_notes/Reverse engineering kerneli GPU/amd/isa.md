## Key question 1 — Official ISA references, machine-readable ISA, "ISA Explorer"

### Takeaway
Every AMD generation the user cares about has a public ISA PDF (RDNA3 Feb-2023, RDNA3.5, RDNA4 dated 7-April-2025, CDNA3, CDNA4, and now CDNA5) plus a machine-readable XML spec with a C++ decoder (isa_spec_manager); the PDFs document encodings, semantics, s_waitcnt/s_wait_* counters, S_DELAY_ALU, VOPD pairing rules, DPP/permlane, WMMA shapes and the LDS bank structure, but give almost no cycle latencies. No separate "ISA Explorer" website was found; the closest things are the `explorer::Spec` API in isa_spec_manager and the ISA views in RGP/RGA.

### Inferences
- For a gfx1100 owner, the practical "spec stack" is: RDNA3 PDF (semantics + hard rules), `amdgpu_isa_rdna3.xml` + IsaDecoder (programmatic decoding of code objects, e.g. to build your own disassembler/annotator, including inside an MCP tool like REA), and LLVM as the executable spec of hazards/latencies.
- The PDFs are explicit about *rules* (VOPD bank rules, delay encoding) but silent about *costs* (cycles per conflict, pipeline depth), which is precisely what must be reverse-engineered empirically (see Q3).

### Gaps
- "AMD GPU ISA Explorer" website: not found on gpuopen.com or in search; only `explorer::Spec` (API) and RGP/RGA ISA views exist. Treat the name as unverified.
- Exact revision strings of RDNA3.5, CDNA3, CDNA4 PDFs were not captured (RDNA3.5/CDNA PDFs not fully inspected; CDNA PDFs undownloadable through the proxy).

## Key question 2 — LLVM AMDGPU backend as a reverse-engineering source

### Takeaway
LLVM is the only *executable* documentation of AMD timing behaviour: `SISchedule.td` encodes per-generation latencies (GFX11: VALU 5, transcendental 10, quarter-rate 8, FP64 38, LDS 20, SMEM 20, VMEM 320, barrier 2000 "cycles"), `GCNHazardRecognizer.cpp` names every hardware hazard, `AMDGPUInsertDelayAlu.cpp` and `SIInsertWaitcnts.cpp` show how S_DELAY_ALU and waitcnts are derived, `GCNCreateVOPD.cpp`/`GCNVOPDUtils.cpp` implement the dual-issue pairing rules, and llvm-mca has GFX11/GFX12 models (`-mtriple=amdgpu11.00`), while `sched_barrier`/`sched_group_barrier`/`iglp_opt` and `amdgpu-waves-per-eu` give hand control over the scheduler and occupancy.

### Inferences
- "What `-O3` does on AMDGPU vs Triton": Triton does *not* bypass LLVM — it runs its own MLIR scheduling (software pipelining `num_stages`, block ping-pong, MFMA hints, buffer ops) and then hands the module to LLVM `O3` with a few function attributes; the final s_waitcnt/s_delay_alu/VOPD decisions are still made by the passes above. So learning the LLVM passes explains both hipcc and Triton output.
- To *see* scheduling decisions for gfx1100: `clang -x hip --offload-arch=gfx1100 -O3 -save-temps` (gives `.s`), `llc -mcpu=gfx1100 -O3 -debug-only=machine-scheduler,si-insert-waitcnts,amdgpu-insert-delay-alu,gcn-create-vopd` on the `.ll` (needs an assertions build of LLVM — unverified availability in ROCm's shipped clang), and `llvm-mca -mtriple=amdgpu11.00` (or `-mtriple=amdgcn -mcpu=gfx1100` on older releases) on the `.s` for per-instruction timeline using the GFX11 model.
- The GFX11 model's numbers are *throughput-derived* ("1/(ops/cycle) + 1 stall"), not measured latencies; they are the compiler's belief, and llvm-mca output inherits that belief (see Q3 for measurements).

### Gaps
- Whether ROCm's shipped `llc`/`clang` is built with assertions (needed for `-debug-only`) was not verified.
- Exact list of valid `-amdgpu-sched-strategy` names was not extracted (observed: `coexec` in Triton, `iterative-ilp` in a Triton comment); check `GCNSchedStrategy.cpp`/`AMDGPUTargetMachine.cpp` `createGCNMaxILPMachineScheduler` family before relying on names.
- The `amdgpu11.00` triple spelling appears in the Oct-2026 main tree; ROCm 7.x releases may still require `-mtriple=amdgcn-amd-amdhsa -mcpu=gfx1100` for llvm-mca.

## Key question 3 — Undocumented / empirically determined behaviour (latencies, LDS banks, VOPD in practice, WMMA throughput, wave32/64, caches, s_setprio, I-cache, VGPR banks)

### Takeaway
The RDNA3 PDF nails the *structure* (4 VGPR banks indexed by SRC[1:0] each with a 3-port register cache, 64×512×32-bit two-port LDS banks per WGP, VOPD rules, S_DELAY_ALU encoding, 20-bit SHADER_CYCLES counter) but gives no cycle costs; measured numbers come from Chips and Cheese (RX 7900 XTX, Jan 2023: FP32 add/FMA latency 5 cycles, INT32 mul ≈ ¼ rate, rcp ¼ rate, dual-issue only clearly seen for `v_dual_add_f32`, scalar cache 15.4 ns, L0 32 KB 32-way, L1 256 KB 16-way, L2 6 MB 16-way, Infinity Cache 96 MB, LDS 128 KB per WGP) and from LLVM's model; the per-conflict VGPR-bank penalty, RDNA3 data-cache line sizes and real WMMA throughput remain unmeasured in public sources and are good targets for the user's own microbenchmarks.

### Cited Findings
- VGPR banks / register cache (RDNA3 PDF §7.6): "There are 4 VGPR banks (indexed by SRC[1:0]), and each bank has a cache. Each cache has 3 read ports: one dedicated to SRC0, one dedicated to SRC1 and one for SRC2. A cache can read all 3 of them at once, but it can't read two SRC0's at once (or SRC1/2)"; VOPD rules: "SRCX0 and SRCY0 must use different VGPR banks; VSRCX1 and VSRCY1 must use different banks; FMAMK is an exception (S1 uses the SRC2 read port); if both operations use SRC2, one must be even and the other odd (FMAMK_F32 second input; DOT2ACC_F32_F16/BF16, FMAC_F32 destination); These are hard rules — the instruction does not function if these rules are broken"; "Dest VGPRs: one must be even and the other odd"; at most 2 SGPRs or 1 SGPR + 1 literal or a shared literal; "Must not use DPP. Must be wave32." — [RDNA3 ISA PDF](https://gpuopen.com/download/rdna3-shader-instruction-set-architecture-feb-2023.pdf)
- RDNA4 PDF §7.8 relaxes one rule: "SRC0x and SRC0y must come from different VGPR banks (bank# = SRC % 4), or be the same VGPR and same sized operand. Same for SRC1"; adds "SCC and EXEC as data count as one SGPR", "V_MOV_B32 when in OPY uses SRC2 (not SRC0) if OPX is also a V_MOV_B32", and clarifies independence: "OPX must not overwrite sources of OPY but OPY may overwrite the sources of OPX without creating a hazard" — [RDNA4 ISA PDF](https://gpuopen.com/download/rdna4-instruction-set-architecture.pdf)
- RDNA3 register cache is hardware-managed; "bank conflicts can reduce achievable register file bandwidth, and impact instruction issue rate" (no cycle figure); RDNA3.5 adds `s_singleuse_vdst`; gfx1151 (Strix Halo) has 192 KB VGPRs per SIMD "like high end RDNA 3", gfx1150 128 KB — [Chips and Cheese RDNA 3.5 LLVM, 2024-02-05](https://chipsandcheese.com/p/amd-rdna-3-5s-llvm-changes); LLVM `Feature1536VGPRs` on gfx1100/gfx1101 (1536 × 32 lanes × 4 B = 192 KB per SIMD) — [GCNFeatures.td](https://github.com/llvm/llvm-project/blob/main/llvm/lib/Target/AMDGPU/GCNFeatures.td)
- Measured on RX 7900 XTX (clocks locked at 1 GHz for per-clock tests), Chips and Cheese 2023-01-07: "common FP operations take 5 cycles on RDNA 3 (Nvidia takes 4)"; INT32 multiply ≈ ¼ FP32 rate with high latency; reciprocal ¼ rate; "Dual issue was clearly seen only for FP32 adds (v_dual_add_f32). INT32 has no VOPD form. FMA rarely dual issued"; VOPD: "operands in the same position cannot read the same bank… three read ports, one per operand position… destination registers cannot be both even or both odd"; Vulkan full-GPU 62.9 TFLOPS FP32 and FP16 FMA; FP64 halved per WGP vs RDNA2 (≈8 → 4 ops/clk); scalar cache 16 KB 4-way, 15.4 ns load-to-use; vector L0 32 KB/CU 32-way; L1 256 KB 16-way per shader array; L2 6 MB 16-way; Infinity Cache 96 MB (down from 128 MB) with higher latency; LDS 128 KB per WGP "built as two 64 KB blocks, each 32 banks of 32-bit", LDS latency "improved greatly"; 384-bit GDDR6 — [Chips and Cheese RDNA3 microbenchmarks](https://chipsandcheese.com/p/microbenchmarking-amds-rdna-3-graphics-architecture)
- RX 7600 (RDNA3): OpenCL FP32 failed to reach full rate "because the compiler did not pack enough operations into VOPD"; forcing wave64 let the code dual-issue, and giving each FMA an immediate input enabled VOPD in wave32 — [Chips and Cheese RX 7600](https://chipsandcheese.com/p/amds-rx-7600-small-rdna-3-appears)
- LDS structure (RDNA3 PDF §12.1, repeated verbatim in RDNA4): "128kB of memory per work-group processor split up into 64 banks of DWORD-wide RAMs… sub-divided into two sets of 32-banks each where 32 of the banks are affiliated with a pair of SIMD32's… Each bank is a 512x32 two-port RAM (1R/1W per clock cycle)… One work-group can request up to 64kB"; "32 store or load instructions [per clock], each nominally 32-bits; load_2addr/store_2addr can be 64-bits"; same-bank concurrent accesses are serialized; LDS ops "can complete in as little as one cycle (for wave32, or 2 cycles for wave64), or take as many as 64 cycles, depending upon the number of bank conflicts" — [RDNA3 ISA PDF](https://gpuopen.com/download/rdna3-shader-instruction-set-architecture-feb-2023.pdf), [RDNA4 ISA PDF](https://gpuopen.com/download/rdna4-instruction-set-architecture.pdf); RGP 2.6+ exposes LDS bank-conflict counters for RDNA3/RDNA4 — [GPUOpen RDT suite update](https://gpuopen.com/learn/radeon-developer-tool-suite-update-rgp-2-6/)
- On CDNA (MI355X) the LDS phase grouping is "non-sequential and undocumented": `ds_read_b128` runs in 4 phases reaching 64 banks, `ds_write_b64` in 4 phases reaching 32 banks; the HipKittens authors wrote solvers to reverse-engineer it and use an XOR swizzle `offset ^= ((offset % 512) >> 7) << 3` for 16x16 bf16 row-layout writes — [Hazy Research "AMD GPUs go brrr", 2025-11-11](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2025-11-09-amd-brr)
- S_DELAY_ALU semantics (RDNA3 PDF §5.7): "optional — not necessary for correct operation"; "may be executed in zero cycles — in parallel with the instruction before it"; encoding `InstID1[4], Skip[3], InstID0[4]` in SIMM16; DEP codes 1–4 = dependent on previous VALU 1–4 back, 5–7 = previous *transcendental* VALU 1–4 back, 9–11 = wait 1–3 cycles for SALU; a new S_DELAY_ALU replaces the previous one's info; "For wave64, the user may not know the status of the EXEC mask and hence not know if instructions take 1 or 2 passes to issue"; must not be used inside VALU clauses (S_CLAUSE) — [RDNA3 ISA PDF](https://gpuopen.com/download/rdna3-shader-instruction-set-architecture-feb-2023.pdf)
- Wave64: "Wave64 waves typically execute each instruction twice" (two passes; both passes use pre-instruction state; one pass can be skipped if EXEC==0 for that half) — [RDNA4 ISA PDF](https://gpuopen.com/download/rdna4-instruction-set-architecture.pdf); VOPD "is legal only for wave32… It is skipped for wave64"; RDNA4 dynamic VGPRs "supported only for wave32" — RDNA3/RDNA4 PDFs above; wave64 "runs a dual-issuable instruction over a single cycle" and RDNA2 pixel shaders often compiled to wave64 "where dual issue needs no extra compiler scheduling" — [Chips and Cheese RDNA3](https://chipsandcheese.com/p/microbenchmarking-amds-rdna-3-graphics-architecture)
- WMMA on RDNA3: 16x16x16 only; "A single WMMA instruction coordinates 32 clocks of optimal work scheduling"; A/B fragments use 8 VGPRs (FP16/BF16), 4 (IU8), 2 (IU4) regardless of wave size; C/D use 8 VGPRs in wave32, 4 in wave64; A column-major, B/C/D row-major; "In wave32, A and B data must be replicated between lanes 0-15 and 16-31 (lane N == lane N+16); in wave64 also into lanes 32-47 and 48-63"; needs ROCm ≥ 5.4 — [GPUOpen WMMA on RDNA3](https://gpuopen.com/learn/wmma_on_rdna3/); ISA: "These instructions work over multiple cycles to compute the result matrix and internally use the DOT instructions" — [RDNA3 ISA PDF](https://gpuopen.com/download/rdna3-shader-instruction-set-architecture-feb-2023.pdf); Chips and Cheese: RDNA3 WMMA "provide the same theoretical throughput as using dot product instructions" — [Chips and Cheese RDNA4 LLVM](https://chipsandcheese.com/p/examining-amds-rdna-4-changes-in-llvm)
- WMMA on RDNA4 (GPUOpen, 2025-07-11): theoretical FLOPS/clock/CU FP16 1024 (RDNA3 512, RDNA2 256), BF16 1024 (512), INT8 2048 (512); RDNA4 "removes that duplication" — each of 32 lanes holds 8 elements so the 16x16 tile is covered exactly once (A/B = 4 VGPRs for 16-bit, C/D = 8 VGPRs); RDNA3 split C/D between lower/upper 16 lanes (even/odd elements) requiring cross-lane shuffles to chain, RDNA4 does not; intrinsics gain `_gfx12` suffix (`__builtin_amdgcn_wmma_f32_16x16x16_f16_w32_gfx12`); RDNA3 code "not backward compatible" — [GPUOpen RDNA4 matrix cores](https://gpuopen.com/learn/using_matrix_core_amd_rdna4/); part 2 fuses two WMMAs for FP8/INT8 "double-K" with 128-bit loads — [GPUOpen RDNA4 WMMA part 2](https://gpuopen.com/learn/wmma-guide-amd-rdna-4-gpus-part-2/); RDNA4 launch analysis: FP16/BF16 matrix throughput 2× RDNA3, INT8/INT4 4×, FP8 (E4M3)/BF8 (E5M2) at INT8 rate, 4:2 sparsity, "Dual-issue capability did not improve", L2 8 MB on RX 9070 XT (2 MB/SE vs 1 MB/SE before), L1 "no longer a dedicated cache… a read/write-coalescing buffer", out-of-order memory accesses, register block spill/fill of 32 regs, dynamic registers — [Chips and Cheese RDNA4, 2025-03-05](https://chipsandcheese.com/p/amds-rdna4-architecture-video)
- RDNA4 WMMA table (ISA §7.12): adds V_WMMA_I32_16X16X32_IU4, V_WMMA_F32_16X16X16_{FP8,BF8}_{FP8,BF8}, and SWMMAC 16x16x32 (F16/BF16/IU8/IU4/FP8/BF8) and 16x16x64_IU4; "Sparse matrices consume half the storage space by requiring that two out of every 4 elements be zero" — [RDNA4 ISA PDF](https://gpuopen.com/download/rdna4-instruction-set-architecture.pdf)
- Caches/lines: RDNA3 I-cache prefetch works in "cachelines (64 bytes)" 1–3 lines ahead of PC (S_SETPRIO-adjacent S_SET_INST_PREFETCH_DISTANCE), and "the user must pad all shaders with 64 extra DWORDs" after s_endpgm because of aggressive prefetch — [RDNA3 ISA PDF](https://gpuopen.com/download/rdna3-shader-instruction-set-architecture-feb-2023.pdf); RDNA4 PDF repeats the 64-DWORD padding rule and gates scalar prefetch behind `SCALAR_PREFETCH_EN` — [RDNA4 ISA PDF](https://gpuopen.com/download/rdna4-instruction-set-architecture.pdf); CDNA3 (MI300X): cache line 128 B, 4 KB TLB pages, Infinity Cache ≈218 ns, Infinity Cache BW 11.9 TB/s measured (17 TB/s theoretical), L2 4 MB per XCD, LDS 64 KB per CU, special functions 4 ops/SIMD/cycle — [Chips and Cheese MI300X, 2024-06-25](https://chipsandcheese.com/p/testing-amds-giant-mi300x)
- s_setprio / s_sleep (RDNA3 PDF): "S_SETPRIO: Set 2-bits of USER_PRIO: user-settable wave priority. 0 = low, 3 = high. Overall wave priority is: {MIN(3,(SysPrio[1:0] + UserPrio[1:0])), WaveAge[3:0]}"; "S_SLEEP: Cause a wave to sleep for approx. 64*SIMM16[6:0] clocks" (RDNA4 adds S_SLEEP_VAR from an SGPR) — [RDNA3 ISA PDF](https://gpuopen.com/download/rdna3-shader-instruction-set-architecture-feb-2023.pdf); HipKittens wraps MFMA clusters in `__builtin_amdgcn_s_setprio(1)` / `s_setprio(0)` and uses `sched_barrier(0)` + `s_barrier` at cluster boundaries — [Hazy Research](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2025-11-09-amd-brr)
- Timing counters for microbenchmarks (RDNA3 PDF): `S_GETREG S0, SHADER_CYCLES` returns a 20-bit cycle counter "not synchronized across SIMDs"; reading it "through the SALU has a typical latency of around 8 cycles"; REALTIME is a constant-rate clock for cross-wave timing — [RDNA3 ISA PDF](https://gpuopen.com/download/rdna3-shader-instruction-set-architecture-feb-2023.pdf); clang exposes `__builtin_amdgcn_s_getreg`, `__builtin_amdgcn_s_memtime`, `__builtin_amdgcn_s_memrealtime` — [BuiltinsAMDGPU.td](https://github.com/llvm/llvm-project/blob/main/clang/include/clang/Basic/BuiltinsAMDGPU.td)
- Register/wave static partitioning on CDNA (MI355X): "With one wave per SIMD, the 512 registers split into 256 AGPRs and 256 VGPRs… registers are statically divided across waves, with no register reallocation", which makes producer/consumer wave specialization lose to an 8-wave ping-pong (two waves per SIMD alternating memory/compute clusters) — 1605 TFLOPS BF16 GEMM at 256x256 tiles vs 1538 (ThunderKittens) / 1570 (CUTLASS) on B200; `buffer_load_dword … lds` gives "TMA-like asynchronous HBM-to-shared loads that bypass the register file"; HIPCC cannot use AGPRs as MFMA inputs (extra accvgpr moves) — [Hazy Research](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2025-11-09-amd-brr), [HipKittens paper arXiv 2511.08083](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08083)

### Inferences
- The LLVM GFX11 latencies (5 / 10 / 8 / 20 / 320) match Chips and Cheese's 5-cycle FP32 measurement and the ISA's "¼ rate" transcendental statements, so for RDNA3 LLVM's model is a reasonable first-order "undocumented latency table"; the LDS = 20 and VMEM = 320 entries are heuristics rather than measurements.
- A VGPR-bank conflict on a 3-operand `v_fma_f32` is only possible across the *same operand slot* of two VOPD halves (hard rule) or when the register cache misses; for single-issue VOPD-free code the PDF implies the 3 ports per bank suffice for one instruction, so the measurable penalty is mostly a VOPD-pairing-failure (lost dual issue), not a stall. This is an inference — no public cycle measurement exists.
- Because RDNA3 WMMA reuses the DOT pipes and duplicates A/B across lane halves, on gfx1100 the matrix path mainly saves instruction-issue slots and VGPR traffic rather than raising peak FLOPs; RDNA4's layout change is why llama.cpp/vLLM RDNA4 FP8 paths need new code.

### Gaps
- No public measurement of the VGPR bank-conflict / register-cache-miss penalty in cycles on RDNA3 (searches 2024–2026 found none).
- No public RDNA3/RDNA4 data-cache line sizes (L0/L1/L2) — Chips and Cheese's RDNA3 article does not report them; only the 64-byte I-cache line (RDNA3 ISA) and 128-byte lines for RDNA4 I-prefetch (from LLVM) / MI300X (measured) are documented.
- Real (measured) WMMA throughput on RX 7900 XTX and RX 9070, and VOPD dual-issue rate for v_dual_fmac in real LLM kernels: not found; the Matrix Instruction Calculator gives theoretical numbers only.
- RDNA3 instruction-cache size/sharing and exact code-size limits were not found in the PDF text (only S_ICACHE_INV and the prefetch window).
- Max waves per SIMD for gfx11 (occupancy ceiling) was not confirmed from a primary source in this pass.

## Key question 4 — Hand-written assembly workflow (inline asm, standalone .s, round trip)

### Takeaway
Clang supports AMDGPU inline asm with `v`/`s`/`a` register constraints (plus `{v[n:m]}`, `{s[n:m]}`, `{exec}`, `{vcc}`, `{m0}`… and immediate constraints `I`, `J`, `A/B/C`, `DA/DB`) and ~860 `__builtin_amdgcn_*` intrinsics, and a standalone kernel is just a `.s` with code + `.amdhsa_kernel` descriptor block + `.amdgpu_metadata` YAML, assembled with `clang -x assembler -target amdgcn-amd-amdhsa -mcpu=gfx1100` and loaded with `hipModuleLoad`; the documented round trip is `hipcc --save-temps` → edit `.s` → reassemble → link with lld → load.

### Cited Findings
- Clang AMDGPU asm constraints (`validateAsmConstraint`): `I` = immediate in [-16, 64] (inline constant range), `J` = immediate in [-32768, 32767], `A`/`B`/`C` = immediates, `DA`/`DB` = immediates, register constraints `v`, `s`, `a`, bracketed forms `{v[n:m]}`, `{s[n:m]}`, `{a[n:m]}`, and special registers `{exec}`, `{vcc}`, `{flat_scratch}`, `{m0}`, `{scc}`, `{tba}`, `{tma}`, `{vcc_lo}`, `{vcc_hi}`, `{exec_lo}`, `{exec_hi}` … — [clang/lib/Basic/Targets/AMDGPU.h](https://github.com/llvm/llvm-project/blob/main/clang/lib/Basic/Targets/AMDGPU.h)
- `BuiltinsAMDGPU.td` lists 860 distinct `__builtin_amdgcn_*` names in the Oct-2026 tree, including (RDNA3-relevant) `__builtin_amdgcn_wmma_f32_16x16x16_f16_w32/w64`, `..._bf16_...`, `..._i32_16x16x16_iu8/iu4_...`, `_tied_` variants, gfx12 `_gfx12` variants and swmmac, `__builtin_amdgcn_mov_dpp`, `update_dpp`, `mov_dpp8`, `permlane16/permlanex16/permlane64`, `readlane/readfirstlane`, `ds_bpermute`, `ds_swizzle`, `ballot_w32/w64`, `s_sleep`, `s_setprio`, `s_barrier`, `wave_barrier`, `s_waitcnt`, `sched_barrier`, `sched_group_barrier`, `iglp_opt`, `s_getreg`, `s_memtime`, `s_memrealtime`, `global_load_lds`, `raw_ptr_buffer_load_lds`, `load_to_lds`, and gfx1250-era `global_load_async_to_lds_b8/b32/b64/b128`, `global_store_async_from_lds_*`, `cluster_load_async_to_lds_*`, `tensor_load_to_lds`/`tensor_store_from_lds`, `s_wait_asynccnt`, `s_wait_tensorcnt`, `global_load_tr*`, `ds_read_tr*` — [BuiltinsAMDGPU.td](https://github.com/llvm/llvm-project/blob/main/clang/include/clang/Basic/BuiltinsAMDGPU.td); the GPUOpen intrinsic form is `__builtin_amdgcn_wmma_<C,D>_16x16x16_<A,B>_w<32|64>(A_frag, B_frag, C_frag, OPSEL)` — [GPUOpen WMMA on RDNA3](https://gpuopen.com/learn/wmma_on_rdna3/)
- Minimal standalone kernel (AMDGPUUsage "Code Object V3 and Above Example Source Code"): `.amdgcn_target "amdgcn-amd-amdhsa--gfx900+xnack"` (optional), `.text` / `.globl hello_world` / `.p2align 8` / `.type hello_world,@function`, the instruction stream ending in `s_endpgm`, then `.rodata` / `.p2align 6` / `.amdhsa_kernel hello_world` with `.amdhsa_user_sgpr_kernarg_segment_ptr 1`, `.amdhsa_next_free_vgpr .amdgcn.next_free_vgpr`, `.amdhsa_next_free_sgpr .amdgcn.next_free_sgpr`, `.end_amdhsa_kernel`, and an `.amdgpu_metadata` … `.end_amdgpu_metadata` YAML block (`amdhsa.version`, `amdhsa.kernels: .name/.symbol hello_world.kd/.kernarg_segment_size/.group_segment_fixed_size/.wavefront_size/.sgpr_count/.vgpr_count/.max_flat_workgroup_size/.args`); `.amdhsa_kernel` "creates a correctly aligned AMDHSA kernel descriptor and a symbol <name>.kd… Only valid when the OS is amdhsa"; required directives `.amdhsa_next_free_vgpr`, `.amdhsa_next_free_sgpr`; other directives include `.amdhsa_wavefront_size32` (GFX10–GFX12), `.amdhsa_workgroup_processor_mode`, `.amdhsa_group_segment_fixed_size`, `.amdhsa_private_segment_fixed_size`, `.amdhsa_kernarg_size`, `.amdhsa_user_sgpr_*`, `.amdhsa_system_vgpr_workitem_id`, `.amdhsa_float_round_mode_*`, `.amdhsa_dx10_clamp`, `.amdhsa_ieee_mode`, `.amdhsa_inst_pref_size`, `.amdhsa_accum_offset` (gfx90a+), `.amdhsa_shared_vgpr_count`, `.amdhsa_code_object_version` — [AMDGPUUsage assembler section](https://llvm.org/docs/AMDGPUUsage.html#code-object-v3-and-above-example-source-code)
- Round-trip commands reported on LLVM Discourse (May 2024): `hipcc -save-temps -O1 --amdgpu-target=<gfx> square.cpp -o square` produces `square-hip-amdgcn-amd-amdhsa-<gfx>.s`; assemble with `/opt/rocm/llvm/bin/clang++ -x assembler -target amdgcn--amdhsa -mcpu=<gfx> -c square.kernel.s -o square.kernel.o`; the host link then fails ("Relocations in generic ELF (EM: 224)") because "the missing step is an lld link" — `hipcc -save-temps -v` prints the real driver steps; pitfalls: inline asm "should only contain instructions and needs explicit register constraints", pasting a whole kernel into `__asm` does not reproduce the compiler's output, SGPR/VGPR metadata must be right, dispatch-pointer changes shift kernarg SGPRs, `v`/`s` constraints suggested by jhuber6 — [LLVM Discourse: Assembling and linking AMD GPU kernel code](https://discourse.llvm.org/t/assembling-and-linking-amd-gpu-kernel-code/78760)
- ROCm blog "Reading AMD GPU ISA" (2024-05-13): `hipcc -c --save-temps -g example.cpp` emits `.s` annotated with source lines; `hipcc -c example.cpp -Rpass-analysis=kernel-resource-usage` prints SGPR/VGPR/AGPR/scratch/LDS/occupancy per kernel at compile time — [ROCm blog](https://rocm.blogs.amd.com/software-tools-optimization/amdgcn-isa/README.html)
- HIP docs: `hipModuleLoad` "loads a code object from a file into a module in the current context" (module API: hipModuleLoad / hipModuleGetFunction / hipModuleLaunchKernel) — [HIP module management](https://rocm.docs.amd.com/projects/HIP/en/docs-6.2.2/reference/hip_runtime_api/modules/module_management.html); HIP compiler docs: hand-written AMDGPU assembly "is uncommon", kernels target one specific GFX arch (gfx1100 = RDNA3) — [HIP compilers](https://rocm.docs.amd.com/projects/HIP/en/docs-7.14.1/understand/compilers.html)
- Older but still-useful assembler repo with `llvm-mc` based examples — [ROCm/LLVM-AMDGPU-Assembler-Extra](https://github.com/ROCm/LLVM-AMDGPU-Assembler-Extra); GPUOpen's "Hsaco from assembly" post (`gpuopen.com/?p=3532`) states "The ability to write code in assembly is essential to achieving the best performance for a GPU program" — [GPUOpen](https://gpuopen.com/?p=3532)
- Production hand-written ASM: AITER "uses Triton, Composable Kernel (CK), and hand-tuned ASM backends" (asm kernels target MI300/MI350; "AITER's C++/ASM kernels do not work on RDNA4") — [ROCm/aiter](https://github.com/ROCm/aiter), [vLLM RDNA4 FP8 thread](https://discuss.vllm.ai/t/native-fp8-wmma-support-for-amd-rdna4-rx-9070-xt-r9700-in-vllm/1900); HipKittens is "productionalized in AITER" — [Hazy Research](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2025-11-09-amd-brr)
- Hazards and inline asm: GCNHazardRecognizer has `checkInlineAsmHazards`, i.e. LLVM tries to pad hazards around inline asm, but ACO's errata list shows several GFX11 hazards need explicit `s_waitcnt_depctr` / `v_nop` that a hand author must insert — [GCNHazardRecognizer.cpp](https://github.com/llvm/llvm-project/blob/main/llvm/lib/Target/AMDGPU/GCNHazardRecognizer.cpp), [mesa README-ISA.md](https://fuchsia.googlesource.com/third_party/mesa/+/098259aa18f70ac3e1baf5b5d7aed073b70114db/src/amd/compiler/README-ISA.md)

### Inferences
- A gfx1100 round trip that follows the documented pieces: (1) `hipcc --offload-arch=gfx1100 -O3 --save-temps k.hip` → `k-hip-amdgcn-amd-amdhsa-gfx1100.s`; (2) edit the `.s` (keep the `.amdhsa_kernel` block and `.amdgpu_metadata` consistent with register use — `.amdhsa_next_free_vgpr/sgpr` are required); (3) `clang -x assembler -target amdgcn-amd-amdhsa -mcpu=gfx1100 -c k.s -o k.o` then `ld.lld -shared k.o -o k.co` (or let clang drive lld: `clang -target amdgcn-amd-amdhsa -mcpu=gfx1100 k.s -o k.co`); (4) `hipModuleLoad(&m, "k.co")`, `hipModuleGetFunction`, `hipModuleLaunchKernel`. Steps 3b/4 are composed from the Discourse thread ("missing lld link") and HIP module docs, not from a single verified tutorial; verify on the machine.
- For inline asm in HIP the pattern is `asm volatile("v_fma_f32 %0, %1, %2, %3" : "=v"(d) : "v"(a), "v"(b), "v"(c));` with `"s"` for SGPR operands and `"memory"` clobbers around waits; LLVM will still insert s_waitcnt/s_delay_alu around the asm but cannot see inside it, so hand-written waits and hazard NOPs inside the asm are the author's responsibility.

### Gaps
- No single official ROCm page documenting the `v`/`s`/`a` inline-asm constraint letters was found; the authoritative source is the clang source file above.
- Exact `ld.lld` flags and `clang-offload-bundler` usage for re-embedding a modified `.s` into a hipcc fat binary were not found in a 2025–2026 source; `hipcc -save-temps -v` is the documented way to discover them.
- No confirmed public gfx1100 `.s` example with `.amdhsa_wavefront_size32 1` and WMMA was found (the AMDGPUUsage example targets gfx900).

## Key question 5 — Hopper/Blackwell-equivalent features on AMD (async global→LDS, TMA-like, barriers, s_sleep, ds_bpermute, wave64)

### Takeaway
AMD's equivalents are uneven: direct global→LDS DMA (`buffer_load … lds` / `global_load_lds`) is the CDNA workhorse (dword on gfx942, up to dwordx4 on gfx950) and is what HipKittens calls "TMA-like"; a real tensor-DMA engine with its own counters (`tensor_load_to_lds`, `s_wait_tensorcnt`, async loads with `s_wait_asynccnt`) only appears in LLVM for gfx1250; RDNA4 adds split barriers (`s_barrier_signal`/`s_barrier_wait`) and dynamic VGPRs, while `ds_bpermute`, `s_sleep`, `s_setprio` and wave64 exist on all RDNA parts.

### Cited Findings
- LLVM defines LDS DMA: "`global_load_lds` or `buffer/global_load` instructions with the `lds` flag are LDS DMA operations" (function attribute `amdgpu-no-lds-dma`); `hasLDSLoadB96_B128()` = gfx950 only ("global_load_lds_dwordx3/dwordx4 or buffer_load_dwordx3/dwordx4 with the lds bit"); sched_barrier has a dedicated 0x0800 LDSDMA class — [AMDGPUUsage](https://llvm.org/docs/AMDGPUUsage.html), [GCNSubtarget.h](https://github.com/llvm/llvm-project/blob/main/llvm/lib/Target/AMDGPU/GCNSubtarget.h)
- HipKittens on MI355X: "`buffer_load_dword` performs TMA-like asynchronous HBM-to-shared loads that bypass the register file… swizzling is done on the HBM addresses rather than the shared-memory addresses" — [Hazy Research](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2025-11-09-amd-brr)
- RDNA3 ISA: "When loading from memory, the data may be loaded into VGPRs first or for some types of loads it may be loaded directly into LDS from memory"; LDS_DIRECT_LOAD/LDS_PARAM_LOAD (LDS→VGPR broadcast, "Direct access is allowed only in CU mode, not WGP mode") are graphics-oriented — [RDNA3 ISA PDF](https://gpuopen.com/download/rdna3-shader-instruction-set-architecture-feb-2023.pdf)

### Inferences
- On the user's RX 7900 XTX there is no TMA and (per LLVM feature gating) no b96/b128 LDS DMA; the practical "async copy" is ordinary `global_load_b128` into VGPRs with deep `s_waitcnt vmcnt(N)` pipelining plus `ds_store_b128`, which is exactly what `SIInsertWaitcnts` and the Triton `num_stages` pipeliner produce. Whether gfx11 supports the dword-sized `buffer_load_dword … lds` form at all should be checked in the RDNA3 MUBUF encoding table before relying on it (see Gaps).
- RDNA4's split barriers + dynamic VGPRs are the closest consumer-AMD analogue to Hopper-style producer/consumer kernels; on RDNA3 ping-pong between two waves per SIMD (HipKittens' 8-wave idea scaled to RDNA's 2 SIMD32 pairs) is the viable structure.

### Gaps
- Whether RDNA3/RDNA4 (gfx11/gfx12) support the MUBUF/global `lds` bit (dword LDS DMA) was not confirmed from the PDFs' encoding tables in this pass; LLVM gates only the b96/b128 forms (gfx950). Treat "LDS DMA on RDNA3" as unverified.
- CDNA3/CDNA4 ISA PDFs could not be fetched (amd.com blocked through the proxy), so MFMA pass counts and `ds_read_b64_tr*` details for gfx942/gfx950 are cited only via LLVM builtins (`__builtin_amdgcn_ds_read_tr*`, `global_load_tr*`, `mfma_*`, `smfmac_*`) and third parties.
